How to Import from India to France: EORI, Duty, TVA and Labels

What a French business needs to register, what it will pay, and how import VAT works in France.

7 MIN READ · FRANCE IMPORT · By Ashu Dhakre

France buys Indian clothing and home linen, leather goods, jewellery, spices and tea, handicrafts, chemicals and engineering parts. Like every EU country, France uses EU customs duty and EU product rules. Two things are more French than European: how import VAT is paid, and the language rules for labels.

1. Get an EORI number

An EORI number is required for any EU import. French businesses request it from French customs (la douane), usually based on their SIRET number. Your customs representative cannot clear goods without it.

2. Find the EU duty rate

Duty is set by the EU and published in the TARIC database. Check your exact product code: spices and many raw materials are low or duty-free, while clothing is often 12%. Some Indian products get reduced GSP rates, but many textile lines do not.

The EU and India concluded negotiations on a free trade agreement in January 2026. Signing is targeted for December 2026, and the agreement still has to be ratified before any duty cuts apply, so for now use the TARIC rate that applies on your import date. Once it is in force, many Indian labour-intensive products such as textiles, leather and footwear are expected to enter duty-free.

3. TVA, and the import VAT reverse charge

France charges TVA at 20% on most goods and 5.5% on most food. Since 2022, VAT-registered businesses in France account for import VAT on their regular VAT return instead of paying it to customs at the border (autoliquidation de la TVA à l'importation). In practice that means no VAT cash outlay when the container lands, which helps cash flow on bigger orders.

The French overseas departments, such as Réunion or Martinique, are outside the EU VAT area and have their own local tax (octroi de mer), so treat them as separate destinations.

4. Product rules and labels

  • French language: product information, labels and instructions for consumer goods sold in France must be in French.
  • Food and spices: EU limits for pesticides, aflatoxins and ethylene oxide apply. Ask for a lab report on your lot before shipment.
  • Textiles and leather: fibre composition labels, plus REACH limits on restricted dyes and chromium VI. France also has its own environmental labelling and recycling (Triman) rules for many consumer products, so check them with your importer.
  • Toys, electricals, cosmetics: CE marking or EU cosmetic notification before sale.

5. Shipping from India to France

Le Havre is the main gateway for Paris and northern France, and Marseille-Fos serves the south. Some French importers also bring goods in through Antwerp or Rotterdam and truck them in. Sea freight usually loads at Nhava Sheva or Mundra. Air cargo to Paris-Charles de Gaulle works for samples and urgent shipments. Compare the options with our CBM and landed cost calculators.

6. Vet the supplier

Check GST and the Import Export Code, do a live factory video call, approve a pre-production sample and inspect before paying the balance. See our supplier verification guide and our page on quality inspection in India.

Not sure where to start? A sourcing agent in India or buying agent can shortlist suppliers, check samples and inspect goods before you pay the balance.

Frequently asked questions

How much is import VAT in France?

The standard TVA rate is 20% and most food is taxed at 5.5%. VAT-registered businesses declare import VAT on their VAT return instead of paying it at customs.

Do product labels need to be in French?

Yes. Labels, product information and instructions for consumer goods sold in France must be in French. Other languages can appear alongside.

Which French port is best for goods from India?

Le Havre is the main port for Paris and northern France, and Marseille-Fos serves the south. The right one depends on where your warehouse is.
