India Supplier Verification Checklist

Use this before you pay a deposit to any supplier in India. It covers the documents to ask for, how to check the factory is real, how to control samples and payment, and the warning signs that usually come before a lost deposit.

How to use it: tick each box as you check it. If you can't tick most boxes in sections 1 to 3, don't pay a deposit yet.

1Company documents

  • GST registration (GSTIN). Check the number on the government GST portal. The legal name and state must match the supplier's invoice and bank details.
  • Import Export Code (IEC). Every Indian exporter needs one from the DGFT. Ask for the number and check it matches the company name.
  • Company or firm registration. For a private limited company, check the record on the MCA (Ministry of Corporate Affairs) site. Note the date of incorporation.
  • Export council membership where relevant: Spices Board, APEDA, Council for Leather Exports, EPCH (handicrafts), CEPC (carpets), Texprocil (cotton textiles). Not a quality guarantee, but a sign of a real exporter.
  • Product licences: FSSAI for food, organic (NPOP) certificates, and any certificate your market needs. Ask for a copy and check the number with the issuing body.
  • Bank account in the company's name. Never pay into a personal account or a third party's account.

2Is the factory real?

  • Live video call from the production floor, not just an office or showroom. Ask them to show machines running, raw material and finished stock.
  • Address check. The factory address on documents should match a real building on Google Maps or Street View.
  • Manufacturer or trader? Ask directly. Traders can be fine, but you should know who actually makes your goods.
  • Capacity. Ask how many units or tonnes they make per month and how long your order will take.
  • Export history. Ask which countries they ship to and for a reference you can contact. Check public trade data if available.

3Samples and specification

  • Written specification agreed before samples: size, material, weight or GSM, colour, grade, packing and labelling.
  • Production sample approved and one sealed sample kept by you (or your agent) as the reference for inspection.
  • Lab tests where needed (food, cosmetics, children's products, leather, dyed textiles), from an accredited lab on a sample from your lot.

4Quote and payment

  • Quote on the same spec from at least two or three suppliers, with Incoterm stated (EXW, FOB, CIF or DDP).
  • Proforma invoice with company name, GSTIN, bank details, product spec, quantity, price, delivery date and payment terms.
  • Staged payment: a deposit (often around 30%) to start, and the balance only after pre-shipment inspection. Avoid 100% advance with a new supplier.
  • Bank details by phone. If bank details "change" by email, confirm by a call to a number you already have. This is a common fraud.

5Before shipment

  • Pre-shipment inspection using AQL sampling, checked against your approved sample and spec.
  • Packing and labels checked: carton marks, barcodes, country of origin and your market's label rules.
  • Documents ready: commercial invoice and packing list that match each other, certificate of origin if needed, and product certificates.

Red flags: stop and check again

  • Price far below every other quote for the same specification
  • Refuses a live factory video call, or only shows a showroom
  • Asks for 100% advance or payment to a personal account
  • Company name on GST, IEC, invoice and bank account don't match
  • Pushes you to decide fast, or changes the spec after you pay
  • Free email address and no website, address or registration you can check
