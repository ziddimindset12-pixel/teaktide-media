Import and sourcing glossary

The trade terms you will meet when buying from India, explained simply.

Sourcing from India comes with a lot of short forms: FOB, AQL, IEC, COA, LCL. Here are 54 terms you will see in quotes, contracts and shipping documents, explained in plain English.

AQL (Acceptable Quality Limit)
A sampling standard (ISO 2859-1) used in inspections. It sets how many defective pieces are acceptable in a random sample before the lot fails. Common levels are 0 for critical, 2.5 for major and 4.0 for minor defects.
Air waybill (AWB)
The transport document for air freight. Unlike a bill of lading it is not a document of title, so the goods are released to the named consignee.
APEDA
Agricultural and Processed Food Products Export Development Authority. Indian exporters of many food and agri products must register with it.
Bill of lading (B/L)
The document a shipping line issues when goods are loaded on a ship. An original B/L is a document of title: whoever holds it can claim the cargo.
BSCI / amfori BSCI
A social compliance audit programme run by amfori, widely used by European retailers to check labour conditions in factories.
CBM
Cubic metre. Sea freight is priced by volume, so you will see CBM on every LCL quote. 1 CBM = 100 × 100 × 100 cm.
Certificate of origin (COO)
A document confirming the country where goods were made. It is needed to claim lower duty under a trade agreement and is often asked for by customs or banks.
CIF (Cost, Insurance and Freight)
Incoterm where the seller pays freight and insurance to your port, but the risk passes to you once the goods are loaded in India.
COA (Certificate of Analysis)
A lab report for a specific batch, showing test results such as moisture, purity, pesticide residues or microbes. Always ask for a COA from the actual lot.
Commercial invoice
The seller's invoice for the shipment. Customs uses it to value the goods, so the description, quantity, value and HS code must be correct.
Consolidation
Combining goods from several suppliers into one shipment or container to save freight.
Customs broker
A licensed agent who files your import declaration and pays duties on your behalf in your country.
DAP (Delivered at Place)
Incoterm where the seller delivers to an agreed place in your country, but you pay import duty and taxes.
DDP (Delivered Duty Paid)
Incoterm where the seller delivers to your door and pays import duty and taxes. Easy for the buyer, but the seller must be able to act as importer in your country.
Demurrage and detention
Charges for keeping a container at the port (demurrage) or outside the port (detention) beyond the free days.
DGFT
Directorate General of Foreign Trade, the Indian government body that issues Import Export Codes and runs export schemes.
Drawback (duty drawback)
A refund of some duties or taxes paid on inputs used to make exported goods. In India it helps exporters keep prices competitive.
EXW (Ex Works)
Incoterm where you collect the goods at the factory and handle everything after that, including Indian export clearance. Rarely the best choice for overseas buyers.
FCL (Full Container Load)
You book a whole container for your goods only. Usually cheaper per CBM than LCL once you are above about 15 CBM.
FOB (Free on Board)
Incoterm where the supplier clears the goods for export and loads them on the ship in India. You pay ocean freight and take the risk from then on.
Freight forwarder
A company that books space with shipping lines or airlines and organises the transport and paperwork for your shipment.
FSSAI
Food Safety and Standards Authority of India. Indian food businesses, including exporters, need an FSSAI licence.
GI tag (Geographical Indication)
A legal label for products linked to a place, such as Darjeeling tea, Mithila makhana or Mysore agarbathi.
GOTS
Global Organic Textile Standard. Certifies organic fibre content and responsible processing for textiles.
GSM
Grams per square metre, the weight of a fabric or towel. Higher GSM usually means a thicker, heavier product.
GST / GSTIN
India's Goods and Services Tax. Every registered Indian business has a 15-character GSTIN you can verify on the government GST portal.
HS code
The Harmonized System code that classifies a product for customs. The first 6 digits are international; countries add more digits for their own tariff.
IEC (Import Export Code)
A 10-character code issued by DGFT. An Indian company needs it to export, so it is one of the first things to check.
Incoterms
Standard trade terms published by the International Chamber of Commerce (current version: Incoterms 2020) that define who pays for and who is responsible for each step of a shipment.
Inspection certificate
A report from an inspection company confirming the goods were checked before shipment. Some buyers make it a condition of paying the balance.
ISF (Importer Security Filing)
Also called 10+2. US importers must file it before goods are loaded on a ship bound for the USA.
Landed cost
The full cost of a product delivered to your warehouse: price, freight, insurance, duty, taxes and local charges.
LC (Letter of Credit)
A bank guarantee that the supplier will be paid once they present the agreed documents. Safer for both sides, but slower and more expensive than a bank transfer.
LCL (Less than Container Load)
Your goods share a container with other shippers. Priced per CBM and good for small orders.
Lead time
The time from confirming an order to the goods being ready to ship. Agree it in writing, including sample approval time.
MOQ (Minimum Order Quantity)
The smallest order a supplier will accept, per product, colour or size. Often negotiable for a first order.
OEKO-TEX Standard 100
A label showing textiles have been tested for harmful substances.
OEM
Original Equipment Manufacturer. The factory makes the product to your design and specifications.
Packing list
A list of what is in each carton: quantities, weights and dimensions. It must match the commercial invoice.
Pre-shipment inspection (PSI)
A check of finished goods at the factory before they ship, usually when at least 80% of the order is packed.
Private label
Selling a manufacturer's product under your own brand, usually with your packaging and small changes to the product.
Proforma invoice (PI)
A quotation in invoice form. It confirms price, quantity, terms and bank details before you pay a deposit.
RFQ (Request for Quotation)
Your written request to suppliers, with the specification, quantity and terms, so their quotes can be compared fairly.
Sample (pre-production / PP sample)
A sample made from the actual materials before bulk production starts. Approve it in writing and keep one as the reference.
SEDEX / SMETA
Sedex is a platform for sharing ethical audit data. SMETA is its audit method, popular with UK retailers.
Shipping marks
The text and symbols printed on cartons, such as your order number, carton number and handling marks.
Spices Board
The Indian government board that registers spice exporters and runs quality labs.
Statement on origin
A declaration by the exporter that goods meet a trade agreement's rules of origin. Used under the India-UK agreement to claim preferential duty.
T/T (Telegraphic Transfer)
A bank wire. The most common way to pay Indian suppliers, usually as a deposit and a balance.
Third-party inspection
An inspection by an independent company such as SGS, Bureau Veritas, Intertek, QIMA or TÜV, booked by the buyer.
Transit time
How long goods take from the port of loading to the port of discharge. India to the US East Coast by sea is often around a month or more.
UFLPA / forced labour rules
Laws such as the US ban on goods made with forced labour. Buyers increasingly ask suppliers to show where raw materials come from.
Volumetric weight
A weight calculated from size, used by airlines and couriers. They charge whichever is higher: actual or volumetric weight.
WRAP
Worldwide Responsible Accredited Production, a social compliance certification common in apparel factories.
